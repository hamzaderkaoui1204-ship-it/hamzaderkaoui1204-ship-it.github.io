How this estimate works

Gravel is the cheapest driveway you can build — often 10% of the cost of concrete or asphalt — but quotes still vary wildly depending on rock type, depth and whether you need a new base or just a top-up. This calculator figures out how many tons you actually need and what the installed cost should look like.

Enter your driveway dimensions and rock type. You will see material, delivery, compaction and optional excavation as separate line items, plus the typical installed price range for 2026.

How we calculate

Volume is computed from area × depth, converted to cubic yards and then tons at 1.4 tons per yard with a 10% compaction factor. Material prices reflect 2026 national averages: crushed limestone ~$28/ton, pea gravel ~$45/ton, asphalt millings ~$18/ton, recycled concrete ~$22/ton, plus a flat delivery fee. Compaction labor is estimated at $0.55 per square foot.

Questions people ask

How many tons of gravel do I need for my driveway?＋

Multiply length × width × depth in feet, divide by 27 to get cubic yards, then multiply by 1.4 tons per yard and add 10% for compaction. A 12×50 driveway at 4 inches needs about 11.6 tons.

What is the cheapest gravel for a driveway?＋

Asphalt millings ($15–$22/ton) and crushed limestone ($25–$35/ton) are the cheapest durable options. Pea gravel costs more and shifts under vehicle weight.

How long does a gravel driveway last?＋

20–100 years with occasional regrading and top-ups every few years. The compacted base does the real work — a proper base lasts decades.

Is gravel cheaper than asphalt or concrete?＋

Yes, dramatically. Gravel runs $1–$3 per square foot installed vs $5–$8 for asphalt and $4.50–$7 for concrete — and repairs are just adding more gravel.

Do I need fabric under a gravel driveway?＋

Geotextile fabric is recommended over clay or soft soil. It costs about $0.10–$0.15 per square foot and prevents the gravel from sinking into the ground.
