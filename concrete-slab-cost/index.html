How this estimate works

A new concrete slab is one of those projects where the final invoice rarely matches the first quote. Material prices swing by region, labor runs $3–$5 per square foot on its own, and extras like reinforcement, vapor barrier and finish work quietly add hundreds. This calculator adds it all up from your actual dimensions so you know what a fair number looks like.

Enter your slab size below and you will get a line-item estimate: ready-mix concrete delivered, reinforcement, forms, vapor barrier and finishing labor — plus the typical price range contractors quote in 2026.

What's included

  • Ready-mix concrete delivered
  • Reinforcement (wire mesh or rebar)
  • Vapor barrier
  • Perimeter forms and stakes
  • Finishing labor (broom, trowel or aggregate)
  • Basic hand tools and supplies

Not included

  • Permits and inspections
  • Excavation, grading and fill dirt
  • Concrete pumping for hard-to-reach pours
  • Frost footings or thickened edges

How we calculate

We estimate from national average 2026 prices: ready-mix concrete at roughly $165 per cubic yard delivered, wire mesh or rebar reinforcement, 6-mil vapor barrier, perimeter forms, and flatwork finishing labor at $2.80–$3.10+ per square foot depending on finish. Questions people ask

How much is a concrete slab per square foot?

A plain 4-inch slab runs $4.50–$7.00 per square foot installed. Thicker slabs, reinforcement, decorative finishes and difficult access push it to $8–$12 per square foot.

How much concrete do I need for a 20×20 slab?

A 20×20 slab at 4 inches thick needs about 5 cubic yards of concrete (roughly 6,800 sq ft-in ÷ 27 × waste factor). At $165 per yard delivered, the concrete alone costs $825–$1,000.

Is it cheaper to pour concrete yourself?

DIY saves labor ($2.80–$4 per sq ft) but you still pay for concrete, forms, reinforcement and tool rentals. It only makes sense for small slabs — a botched pour costs more to remove than a pro charge.

Does a 6-inch slab cost much more than 4 inches?

Yes — roughly 50% more concrete by volume, plus rebar is standard at 6 inches. Expect $1.50–$2.50 more per square foot.

How long does a concrete slab last?

A properly poured and cured slab lasts 50+ years. Control joints, good base compaction and reinforcement matter more than the brand of cement.
