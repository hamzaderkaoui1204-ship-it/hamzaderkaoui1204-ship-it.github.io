How this estimate works

Painting a room looks cheap until the quote arrives: $300–$800 per room is the national norm, and a whole interior runs $2–$6 per square foot of wall. Paint itself is often the smallest line — labor is 70–80% of the bill. This calculator shows exactly where the money goes.

Enter your room dimensions, doors and windows, then pick a paint grade. You will get gallons needed, paint and primer cost, supplies and professional labor as separate 2026 line items.

Not included

  • High ceilings over 10 ft

How we calculate

Wall area = perimeter × ceiling height, minus 20 sq ft per door and 15 sq ft per window. One gallon covers ~380 sq ft per coat. Paint is priced at $20/gal (contractor), $35 (builder) or $60 (designer). Professional labor is estimated at $2.90/sq ft of wall and $2.00/sq ft of ceiling.

Questions people ask

How much does it cost to paint a room professionally?

Most pros charge $300–$800 per standard bedroom including paint, or $2–$6 per square foot of wall. Ceilings, trim and dark colors pushed over light add 20–40%.

How much paint do I need for one room?

A 12×12 room with 8-ft ceilings has ~380 sq ft of wall — one gallon per coat. Two coats plus primer: budget 3 gallons, or 2 if repainting the same color.

Is it cheaper to paint yourself?

DIY costs $100–$200 in materials per room vs $400–$800 for a pro. Your time is the trade: a first-timer needs 2–3 days per room vs a pro's half day.

Do I need primer if I am repainting?

Not when repainting a similar color in good condition. Primer is mandatory over repairs, stains, bare drywall, or when going from dark to light.

Why do painters charge more for dark walls?

Dark colors need 3–4 coats or a tinted primer to cover evenly — that is 50–100% more labor and paint over a light base.
