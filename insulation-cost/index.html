How this estimate works

Adding attic insulation is the highest-ROI home upgrade there is — often paying for itself in 2–5 years of heating and cooling savings. The Department of Energy recommends R49–R60 for most US climates, and most attics are sitting at R19 or less. The math below shows what topping up should cost.

How we calculate

Cellulose provides ~R3.7 per inch and costs about $0.18/sq ft per inch installed-material. Added depth = target depth (R38≈13in, R49≈16in, R60≈19in) minus current depth. Blower rental runs ~$105/day (often free with material purchase), pro labor $0.48/sq ft, air sealing $0.25/sq ft.

Questions people ask

How much does it cost to insulate a 1,000 sq ft attic?

Blowing in R-49 cellulose over existing insulation costs $1,100–$1,900 installed. DIY with a big-box store blower rental runs $500–$900 in material.

How many inches of insulation is R-49?

Cellulose: about 16 inches. Fiberglass batts: roughly 14 inches of batt. Blown fiberglass needs ~18–19 inches to hit R-49.

What R-value should my attic have?

R-38 in warm climates (zones 1–2), R-49 across most of the country, R-60 in the coldest northern states. Check the DOE zone map for your county.

Is attic insulation worth the cost?

Usually the best money you can spend on a home: the DOE estimates a 10–50% cut in heating/cooling bills, with payback in 2–5 years in most climates.

Can I add new insulation over old?

Yes, if the old insulation is dry and uncontaminated. New cellulose goes right over it. If it is wet, compressed, or has rodent damage, removal ($1–$2/sq ft extra) comes first.
